The Data Room

Twelve free, interactive explorers built on India's own official surveys and open academic data. Read on their own, each answers a plain-language question about the country. Read together, they trace one continuous life-course — from a child's first reading test to the wage economy, the smaller cities where it increasingly happens, the ballot box, the caste line drawn at the kitchen door — and, at the end, how the whole endeavour weighs on the planet.

  • 19 explorers
  • 16 sources
  • State → district where the data allows
  • Every figure validated & source-linked
Free forever · no login · no paywall · the government's own numbers
Why one room? A single survey is a snapshot. Placed side by side, these nineteen become a story with a spine — the same child who sits in an under-equipped classroom (UDISE+), struggles to read a simple paragraph (ASER), drifts further from the syllabus each year (NAS) and mostly never reaches college (AISHE) grows into the worker counted by PLFS, the migrant traced in the Census — increasingly bound for the Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities the LEAP index scores — and the voter tallied by the Election Commission — all the while carrying the caste line the IHDS still finds drawn at the kitchen door, and all of it running on the power grid the Energy explorer maps — while the Happy Planet Index asks what all of it costs the planet, and what it delivers in life. Pick your own state or district and read your own numbers.

How these are built. Every explorer draws on the official published survey — or, for the LEAP Cities Explorer and the HPI Explorer, on openly published academic datasets (Zenodo CC BY 4.0; the Hot or Cool Institute's public data file, cited as it requests) — no scraping of unofficial mirrors passed off as source, no invented figures. Where a number is derived (e.g. a district-level average assembled from official learning-outcome figures), the explorer says so plainly and shows how close it lands to the published total. If a figure can't be sourced and validated, it isn't shown. The Scheme Tracker follows the same rule with a different pairing — an official government claim beside an official audit finding (the CAG, a parliamentary committee, or official data), both cited, no commentary.
