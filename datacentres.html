Data Centres Explorer

India is courting data centres with subsidies, cheaper power and faster approvals. This explorer shows where the facilities are, who runs them, which states pay to attract them, and where the coal plants that power them sit. It uses the facility inventory that Priyanka deSouza and colleagues built for their 2026 study of who bears the environmental cost.

Inventory to 30 September 2026.

  • 233 operating
  • 60 under construction
  • 36 announced
  • 61 districts host the operating ones
  • 13 states with a dedicated policy
Source: deSouza, Paul, Mathew, Lippert, Ratti and Duarte (2026), The Environmental Geography of Data Centers in India, SSRN preprint · public data on GitHub · CC BY-NC 4.0 · inventory as of 30 September 2026

What the study asks

A data centre draws industrial amounts of electricity and cooling water around the clock and employs few people. In several countries that has produced local opposition and moratoria. Indian states are moving the other way and competing for the investment. The study asks a question nobody had answered for India: when a data centre is built, who breathes the pollution and whose water is drawn?

Its answer is that the facility and its burden sit in different places. The buildings cluster in wealthy, urban districts. The electricity comes largely from coal plants in poorer, more rural districts, and the pollution from those plants spreads over most of the country.

What the authors found

  • 86%of operating facilities are in states that offer data-centre incentives
  • 62%lie in river basins with extremely high baseline water stress
  • about 9%of India's district-assigned coal capacity is in the districts that host data centres
  • ~1.3 billionpeople live where the modelled electricity-related pollution reaches
  • US$2.5 to 3.9 bna year in modelled health and carbon costs, or US$127 to 192 per MWh consumed
  • 20 TWha year of electricity demand today, 1.1% of national generation

These six figures are the authors' results as stated in the paper's abstract. The pollution, health and cost figures are model estimates that rest on assumed loads for facilities that publish none. Nothing on this page recomputes them.

Reading this page with care

What counts as a facility. The inventory lists 329 sites as of 30 September 2026: 233 operating, 60 under construction and 36 announced. The authors built it from the Data Center Map directory, operators' own facility lists, government records and the press, and checked each row against its source. They call the count of operating sites conservative, and they keep a separate list of 75 sites that public sources neither confirm nor refute. Those 75 are not shown here.

Confidence differs by row. The authors grade each facility. Of the 233 operating sites, 119 are graded high, 29 medium and 85 low. A low grade usually means the site appears in a directory and no independent source confirms its current status. The list view shows the grade for every row.

Capacity is mostly unpublished. Only 123 of the 233 operating facilities publish an IT load. For the other 110 the authors model a load in order to estimate pollution, and they describe that figure as an allocation. This page shows a capacity only where one was published, and says "not published" everywhere else. A total across operators therefore covers some sites and not others, and each total says how many.

Location precision. Coordinates are exact to the building for some sites and only to the locality or city for others. Marks that share a spot on the map may be separate buildings in one campus or several sites placed at one city centre.

The power plants. The grey and yellow squares are the 253 coal and 68 gas plants from the WRI Global Power Plant Database that the authors use. The map shows that the plants and the data centres occupy different parts of the country. It does not show which plant supplies which facility. The grid is pooled, and the paper says the same.

The incentives table. The authors reviewed 18 states and found a dedicated data-centre policy in 13. Each entry was checked against the policy document or a credible report of it on 1 September 2026. "Partial" means the incentive exists with conditions.

Boundaries. State outlines are dissolved from DataMeet's Census 2011 district map, so Telangana is drawn inside Andhra Pradesh and Ladakh inside Jammu and Kashmir. Facility records use today's state names.
