How to read it

Empowerment is the expansion of the ability to make strategic life choices, in a context where that ability was previously denied

That is Kabeer’s definition, and every word in it is load-bearing. Strategic excludes the second-order choices a person may make freely while the first-order ones are settled for them. Ability to choose requires that an alternative existed, that the person could see it, and that choosing carried consequence. Previously denied means empowerment is a change over time, so a snapshot cannot show it.

The three parts are a sequence and not a menu. Resources are the pre-conditions, agency is the process, achievements are the outcomes, and a measurement of any one alone can move for reasons the framework would not count.

The half of the paper that gets dropped

Kabeer’s 1999 article is cited constantly for the three words and rarely for what follows them, which is a long and specific warning about proxies. Her argument is that the standard measures each fail in a characteristic way: a resource can be delivered to a woman and controlled by someone else; agency asked about in a questionnaire cannot see the decisions nobody raised; and an achievement can improve because circumstances changed rather than because anyone chose.

The sharpest of her cautions is about preference. A person who has adapted to a constraint reports the constraint as a preference, so an instrument that asks what someone wants will record the adaptation as a want. That is not a technical problem to be corrected with a better question; it is a limit on what asking can establish.

This page is that warning, run against Indian data. Every indicator here is one a gender report would quote, every one moves in the direction the report would want, and every one has a companion in the same survey that changes what it means.

Whose framework this is

What this page does not do

It does not claim these indicators are worthless, or that nothing has improved. Married women reporting a say in their own health care rose from 62.2 per cent to 81.1, and women reporting no say in any of the three decisions fell from 23.5 to 11.3. Those are large movements in a consistent direction across three rounds.

The claim is about what a single series can establish, not about whether India has changed

Using this in a workshop

Bring the indicators your own programme reports on gender, and for each one ask the two questions this page asks. Which of the three parts is it measuring? And what is the companion series in the same instrument that would complicate it?

The second question is the useful one, because the answer is usually that nobody has looked. Most gender reporting quotes one series per claim, and most instruments carry a second series that bears on it directly. The twelve-month violence figure sits in the same NFHS table as the lifetime figure.

Read alongside Practical and Strategic Gender Needs, which is about what interventions do rather than what empowerment is, and the Results Chain, which is about the opposite failure: an outcome nobody measures at all.

If you reproduce the framework, credit Kabeer. If you reproduce the pairings or the readings, credit ImpactMojo, and argue with them.
