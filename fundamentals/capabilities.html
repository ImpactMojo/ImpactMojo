How to read it

98.1% of rural children are enrolled. 44.8% of Std V children in government schools can read a Std II text.

Both figures are from the same survey in the same year. The first counts a resource; the second asks about a capability. Sen’s argument is that development gets measured almost entirely in the first kind of number, and that the two can move apart for reasons that are neither accidental nor mysterious.

A resource is countableSchool places, toilets, cycles, grain. This is what budget lines and targets are written in, because it can be counted before anyone has used it.
A conversion factor stands in betweenSen groups them three ways: personal, social and environmental. They decide how much freedom a given resource yields, and they differ by person and by place.
A capability is what you can actually doThe real opportunity, whether or not it is taken up. This is the stage almost no administrative dataset records.

Where the approach is contested

Its most persistent criticism is that it resists measurement. Sen declined for decades to publish a fixed list of capabilities, holding that the selection is a public and political judgment rather than a philosopher’s. Martha Nussbaum disagreed and published ten. That disagreement is unresolved, and both positions are set out below rather than merged.

A second objection is practical: if capability is hard to observe and resources are easy to count, a government under pressure to report progress will keep reporting resources. The approach diagnoses the problem more comfortably than it solves it.

And a capability is not a functioning. Someone who fasts and someone who is starving have the same nutritional outcome and entirely different freedom. The distinction matters most exactly where it is hardest to observe.

The other half of the argument

Nussbaum’s ten, and why Sen would not write them

Martha Nussbaum argued that an approach with no list cannot ground a constitutional claim, and published ten central capabilities she holds every government owes its people at a threshold level. Sen’s objection was never to these ten in particular; it was that fixing any list removes from public reasoning a choice that belongs to it.

    Why this matters in Indian practice

    The Indian Constitution already reads much like a capability list. The Directive Principles name livelihood, health, education and a living wage; the Supreme Court has read the right to life in Article 21 to include food, shelter, health, a clean environment and sanitation. India did not need Nussbaum’s list to arrive at a threshold account of what the state owes.

    What the approach adds is the question of what a guarantee is measured in. A right to education discharged in school places and a right to education discharged in reading are the same right and a different programme.

    Using this in a workshop

    Take a programme the group works on and write down what it provides. Then ask what a person would have to be able to do for that provision to have worked, and what stands between the two for the hardest-placed person it serves. The gap between those two sentences is usually where the programme’s evaluation should have been pointed.
