Teacher Evidence Explorer

Filter teacher-effectiveness interventions by what the evidence actually says — quality, cost, India relevance, and outcome. Built from rigorous evaluations 2000–2024 across India and comparable contexts.

What this is — and what it isn't

An organised view of 30+ teacher interventions that have been rigorously studied. Each card includes: what the intervention is, where it has been tested, what the evidence quality is, what it costs (per teacher per year, India context), and what the strongest studies actually found. Use the filters to focus.

This is not a ranking. A "Strong evidence + Low cost" intervention is not automatically better than a "Moderate evidence + High cost" one — context, capacity, and goals matter. Use the explorer to find the intervention that fits your problem, not the one with the prettiest badge.

Top-line patterns across this evidence base

If you scroll the full list, a few patterns emerge clearly:

  • Pedagogy interventions (TaRL, structured pedagogy, scripted lessons) consistently outperform pure incentive interventions for learning outcomes, at much lower cost.
  • Pure teacher pay-for-performance has the weakest evidence in India — Muralidharan & Sundararaman (2011) showed effects, but more replications have shown null or mixed results, especially when scaled. Be skeptical of "just pay better teachers more."
  • Cascade teacher training (training the trainer of the trainer) typically loses 60–80% of fidelity by the time it reaches the classroom. Direct training of teachers + ongoing mentoring outperforms cascade by large margins.
  • Contract teachers (Para-teachers) show positive learning effects at substantially lower cost than government teachers — but raise serious equity and rights concerns. The evidence here is real and politically uncomfortable; treat it carefully.
  • Mentoring + coaching outperforms one-time workshops across nearly every well-designed study. The single most under-funded teacher intervention in India.
  • Education technology in classrooms (computer-assisted learning, tablets, adaptive software) shows mixed results — positive when complementing teaching, often null or negative when substituting for it. Mindspark India had clear effects (Muralidharan, Singh, Ganimian 2019); many tablet rollouts have not.
  • Multi-grade pedagogy training is one of the most overlooked high-evidence interventions for rural India, where most government primary schools have multi-grade classrooms.

For the deeper methodological discussion behind these findings, see the SEL Evaluation in India deep dive and the MEL flagship course.
