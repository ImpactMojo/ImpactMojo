Why an annotated bare Act?

The bare text of a law is free, but it is written for courts, not for the field. A programme officer facing a stonewalling PIO does not need the whole Act; they need to know which section is their lever, and how to pull it.

This guide keeps the two things cleanly separated. The statute is exact: transcribed verbatim from the authoritative India Code version, so you can rely on and cite it. The annotations are ours: a plain-language gloss and a practitioner's note written for the development sector, RTI as the spine of a social audit, §6(2) as your shield against a nosy officer, §8's public-interest override, §20's penalty as leverage in appeal. It is the difference between owning the law and being able to use it.

What's inside: the seven sections you actually use

  • § 3: Right to information. The one-sentence foundation, and why "citizen" shapes how an NGO files.
  • § 4: Obligations of public authorities. The 17 categories a department must publish anyway, and how the gaps become your advocacy point.
  • § 6: Request for obtaining information. How to ask, the "no reason required" shield, and the five-day transfer rule.
  • § 7: Disposal of request. The 30-day clock, the 48-hour life-or-liberty track, deemed refusal, and fee-inflation as a soft denial.
  • § 8: Exemption from disclosure. Where refusals hide, and the public-interest override and personal-information limits that beat most of them.
  • § 19: Appeal. First and second appeals, the timelines, and the reversed burden of proof.
  • § 20: Penalties. ₹250/day, up to ₹25,000: the enforcement tooth, and how to use it as leverage.

§ 3Right to information
Right to information.: Subject to the provisions of this Act, all citizens shall have the right to information.
● For the development sector

This is the legal spine of every social audit and accountability drive. The word is "citizens": an organisation cannot file in its own name, so your RTIs go out under an individual (a staff member, or better, an affected community member with your drafting support). It is the lever for muster rolls, beneficiary and ration-card lists, sanction letters and fund-utilisation statements: the raw material for checking whether a scheme actually reached people.

FAQ

Is the statutory text reliable?

Yes. The Act is reproduced verbatim from the authoritative India Code version (Act 22 of 2005), including the 2023 amendment to §8(1)(j) by the Digital Personal Data Protection Act. Nothing is paraphrased. The annotations are clearly marked as ImpactMojo's own commentary.

Is this legal advice?

No. The annotations are practitioner commentary to help you understand and use the Act in development work: they are not legal advice, and do not create a lawyer–client relationship. For a specific dispute, consult a lawyer.
