India had 319 districts at the 1951 census, and the government's directory listed 790 in November 2026. A district-level comparison across two years is only valid if the district covered the same ground in both. Reading this page with care

What the crosswalk is. India State Stories publishes a table with one row for each line of descent: a district in the 2024 list, and the district that held its territory at each census from 1951 to 2011. A district formed from parts of two older districts has two rows. The source has 805 such rows. This page carries each of them forward to the 2026 directory, as described below.

What "add these together" means. For any two years, the page groups districts that share territory. If one 2011 district became three by 2024, the three 2024 districts together cover the 2011 district, and that group is the smallest unit you can compare across the two years. This grouping is ImpactMojo's calculation from the crosswalk. It is exact for whole-district splits and mergers.

What it cannot see. A split usually moves some tehsils or blocks, and the crosswalk records parent and child only. When a new district took a part of a second district, the group includes both parents in full, which is the safe choice and sometimes a larger group than strictly needed. Transfers of a few villages between districts that kept their names are not recorded at all.

The 2026 column is the government's own list. The Local Government Directory is the register the Ministry of Panchayati Raj keeps of every state, district, block and village, and each district in it has a code that government datasets use. This page uses the directory's district list of , which has districts, taken from a public daily archive of the directory. When you look up a district, the 2026 row shows its directory code and, where the directory records one, its Census 2011 code.

How the 2026 column was joined, which is ImpactMojo's work. The source's tables end at a 2024 list of 760 districts. For each of those, this page found the same district in the directory. Most match by name; are matched by hand because the two lists spell or name them differently, for example Karimganj, which the directory lists under its new name Sribhumi. The directory also has districts that the 2024 list lacks. For each one, the parent districts were read from a state notification, an official release or a news report, and the full list with its sources is below. A wrong parent would give a wrong group, so check the source if a result surprises you.

Rajasthan. The source's 2024 column lists 33 districts for Rajasthan, the position before August 2023. The 2026 column has the 41 districts that remain, so use it for any current comparison. With the 2024 column, a Rajasthan district that was divided in 2023 is marked "check before comparing".

Survey rounds fall between these years. The columns are census years, the source's 2024 list and the 2026 directory. NFHS-4 used the 640 districts of Census 2011. NFHS-5 used 707 districts as they stood in March 2017, which is between the 2011 and 2024 columns. For such a round, use the dated split records under each district to see which changes had happened by then.

Checks against the census. The crosswalk's district counts equal the Census of India's for 2011 (640), 1991 (466), 1981 (412) and 1971 (356). For 2001 it has 592 against the census's 593.

What was changed in the source. Spellings are kept as the source prints them, so the same district can appear as Bathinda in one year and Bhatinda in another. Nine records print a year that lies outside the decade they are filed under; they are shown as printed and marked.
