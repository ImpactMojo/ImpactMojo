Development Law Docket

Indian judgments that changed what development organisations, state programmes and rights-holders can do. Each entry says what the court decided, what changed in practice and whether the ruling is still good law.

This is orientation, not legal advice. Holdings here are summarised editorially. The linked judgment is the authority, so read it before relying on it. Case law moves: a later bench can narrow a holding, a statute can replace it, or a larger bench can reconsider it. Every entry carries a status that says which, but statuses go stale. Check the date, then check the source.

What is deliberately not here

    How these entries were checked

    Coverage is the Supreme Court of India, the High Courts, the tribunals and the consumer commissions. Use the court filter above to see what is here from each.

    District court decisions are not included. A district court decision binds only the parties in front of it, so it cannot settle what an organisation elsewhere may or must do, and that is what this docket collects. Most of what a district court decides is also private family and criminal matters. Where a district-level forum does decide what somebody is owed, such as a consumer commission, a claims tribunal or a labour court, that belongs here.
