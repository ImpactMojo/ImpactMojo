NAS Learning Explorer

What do children in India's government schools actually know? The National Achievement Survey (NAS) tests Classes 3, 5, 8 and 10 on the school curriculum — language, maths, science, social science — and this ranks how each state does. The curriculum-aligned companion to the ASER household survey: ASER asks whether a child can read at all; NAS asks how much of the syllabus they've mastered.

What is NAS — and why it matters

Once every few years, NCERT tests a huge sample of government and government-aided school children — 3.4 million students in 2021 — on what the curriculum says they should have learned by Class 3, 5, 8 and 10. The score is the average percentage of questions answered correctly. It is the official, curriculum-referenced counterpart to ASER's simpler "can they read a Std II text" test — and the two tell a consistent, sobering story: performance falls sharply as children move up the grades, from about 60% correct in Class 3 to the mid-30s by Class 10. NAS is the number education departments are held to.

Making sense of the numbers

What the score means. Each figure is the average share of test questions a state's Class-3/5/8/10 students answered correctly in NAS 2021, for that subject. It is a curriculum-referenced measure — "how much of what you were supposed to learn did you learn" — unlike ASER, which measures a single foundational floor (can the child read a Std II paragraph, do a subtraction). Both matter; read together, ASER tells you if the floor is missing and NAS tells you how far short of the ceiling a state falls.

Reading it responsibly. NAS covers government and government-aided schools, so it says little about the private sector where a large share of Indian children now study. The steep grade-on-grade decline is the headline finding — a Class 10 cohort scoring in the 30s is a system-wide signal, not a single state's failing. And a percentage-correct score depends on how hard that year's paper was, so compare states within a class and subject, against the All-India line, rather than across classes as if they were the same test.

State, district and skill. State scores are the official NAS 2021 subject figures, validated against the published National Report Card (Class 3 language 61.9%, Class 8 maths 36.0%, Class 10 maths 32.1%). District scores (the "zoom into the districts" view) are derived: rolled up from the official district learning-outcome data, they reproduce the exact subject scores within about half a point on average (median 0.4pt; a few cells differ by up to ~4pt), and are labelled as a derivation, not the exact IRT-scaled official number. The "break down the skills" view is the opposite — it shows the exact, official district performance on each individual learning outcome, no aggregation. Together: state and skill are exact; the district ranking is a close, honest approximation across all 720 districts.
