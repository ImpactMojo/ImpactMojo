1. What this model is

Intervention A treats severely malnourished children as inpatients (or day-care patients) at Nutrition Rehabilitation Centres (NRCs) co-located in Primary and Community Health Centres. Children stay for a supervised therapeutic-feeding course with daily clinical monitoring, then are discharged to follow-up. The model is staff- and facility-intensive, which is why its per-child unit cost is high, but it concentrates resources on the most severe (SAM) cases and achieves strong recovery.

3. Fixed vs variable split (for scale-up modelling)

Roughly 70% of the unit cost is fixed: staff, facility and equipment that must be in place whether the NRC ward is full or half-empty. Only ~30% (therapeutic food, supplies, case-level M&E) scales directly with the number of children treated. This matters for the 15-district scale-up: facility-based costs do not fall much per child unless bed-occupancy is kept high.

4. Pilot total (illustrative)

Over the 18-month pilot the NRCs treated 2,400 children with an average stay of about 2.9 months, giving roughly 6,845 child-treatment-months. At ₹8,500 per child-month that is an indicative pilot spend of about ₹5.82 crore. Use the month-by-month figures in the outcome dataset to derive your own active-caseload and cost series rather than relying on this round figure.

Note for modellers: the unit cost is expressed per child per treatment-month, not per enrolled child. Because the average length of stay is under three months, the cost per child reached is higher than ₹8,500, and the cost per child recovered is higher still, since 32% of admissions do not recover. Deriving cost per child recovered is a core part of the task.
