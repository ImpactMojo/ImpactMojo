↧ Download raw data (CSV)
How to use this dataset. This is the core data file for the challenge “Cost-Effectiveness Analysis of Two Nutrition Interventions”. Pair it with the Intervention A and Intervention B cost breakdowns. The CSV has one row per month × intervention (36 rows). Compute your own cost-effectiveness ratios, severity-adjusted recovery, and 15-district scale-up scenarios.

Column dictionary

  • monthpilot month, 1–18.
  • interventionA_facility (NRC) or B_community (ASHA counselling).
  • new_enrolmentschildren newly enrolled that month.
  • children_activechildren under active treatment/follow-up that month (the caseload that drives monthly cost).
  • intake_SAM / intake_MAM: of that month's new enrolments, those classified severe (SAM) vs moderate (MAM) acute malnutrition at intake. SAM + MAM = new_enrolments.
  • outcome_recovered / outcome_improved / outcome_no_change / outcome_defaulted: treatment outcome for that month's enrolment cohort. The four sum to new_enrolments.
  • monthly_cost_inrchildren_active × unit cost (₹8,500 for A, ₹1,200 for B).

Questions to push your analysis

  • Naive cost per child recovered is about ₹35,650 for A and ₹11,020 for B. How much of that 3× gap survives once you adjust for intake severity (A treats far more SAM cases)?
  • How do you treat the improved column: partial credit, or only count full recovery?
  • B defaults on ~24% of cases vs A's ~8%. What does that imply for effective reach and for a blended design?
  • Scaling to 15 districts: A's 70% fixed cost is sensitive to bed-occupancy; B's cost is near-linear in volume. Model both.
