Nirmal Neer Programme: Our Impact This Year

Piped water & hygiene promotion · 220 villages across Jhansi & Lalitpur (UP), Tikamgarh & Chhatarpur (MP): Bundelkhand

Reproduced verbatim from the published report · Section: “Evidence of Change” · Approved by: Board, 21 May 2025
This extract is exactly as published. Nothing has been annotated or corrected. Your job in the challenge is to audit the headline claim and every step of the measurement behind it, then design a better evaluation using the FY 2026–27 rollout plan.
“Our flagship result this year: a 43% reduction in child diarrhoea episodes, attributable to the Nirmal Neer Programme, proof that safe water at the doorstep transforms child health.” , From the Chairperson’s foreword, JalSeva Trust Annual Report 2024–25

Since 2021, JalSeva Trust has brought functional household tap connections, source strengthening and hygiene promotion to 220 villages of Bundelkhand, working alongside gram panchayats and village water and sanitation committees. This year, for the first time, we can report rigorous evidence of the health change our supporters have made possible.

How we measured

  • Design: Baseline–endline comparison in 118 participant villages where the full package (tap connections + hygiene sessions) was operational for at least 12 months.
  • Selection: Participant villages joined the programme after their gram panchayat submitted a written application and passed a community-contribution resolution.1
  • Rounds: Baseline survey in September 2023; endline survey in March 2025.2
  • Sample: 2,832 households with at least one child under five at baseline; 2,590 households re-contacted at endline.
  • Instrument: Caregiver recall of diarrhoea episodes (three or more loose stools in 24 hours) in the preceding four weeks, converted to episodes per child per month.
  • Enumeration: Surveys administered by our trained village-level Jal Mitras and block coordinators, keeping costs low and building staff capability.3

The 43% fall in diarrhoea episodes across 118 villages is consistent across both districts’ blocks and both age groups. On this basis the Trust attributes the improvement to the Nirmal Neer package, and we are confident the remaining 102 programme villages are seeing similar gains as their schemes mature.

Voices from the field

“Earlier my daughter fell ill every month in the rains. Since the tap came, she has not missed school once,” says Ramkali Devi of Bamhori village, Talbehat block. Our Jal Mitras report that families now proudly show them their handwashing stations during monthly visits.

Notes to the “Evidence of Change” section

1 The application route ensures we work where communities are motivated: applicant panchayats committed 5% capital cost contribution and formed water committees before works began. 118 of our 220 villages had completed 12+ months of the full package by March 2025 and were therefore surveyed.

2 The baseline was fielded in the weeks following the 2023 monsoon withdrawal; the endline was fielded before the onset of summer 2025, once scheme handover formalities in the final batch of villages concluded.

3 Using programme staff as enumerators saved an estimated ₹9.4 lakh versus a third-party survey agency, funds we redirected to tap connections for 310 additional households. Jal Mitras received a one-day refresher on the questionnaire before each round.

4 We acknowledge the wider context: during 2023–25 the Swachh Bharat Mission (Grameen) Phase II ODF-Plus drive intensified across all our operational blocks, with sustained IEC campaigns on sanitation and handwashing led by district administrations. Our hygiene sessions complemented these state efforts.

5 The 2024 south-west monsoon was deficient in Bundelkhand (roughly 28% below the long-period average in our districts). Several traditional surface sources, ponds and open wells that households previously relied upon, remained dry for much of the year, underlining the value of piped supply.
