1. Background & rationale

HealthBridge Foundation is a mid-sized NGO working on rural health systems strengthening in central India. In 2022 it secured a three-year grant to reduce neonatal mortality (deaths in the first 28 days of life) across three high-burden districts of Madhya Pradesh: Sheopur, Shivpuri and Guna.

These districts sit well above the state's neonatal mortality rate. Most newborn deaths are concentrated in the first week of life and are driven by preventable causes: low rates of institutional delivery, weak birth preparedness, poor recognition of newborn danger signs, and delays in referral. Antenatal care (ANC) coverage is low and uneven, and front-line workers (ASHAs and ANMs) report limited confidence in newborn-care counselling.

2. Goal and intended change

The programme's stated goal, taken verbatim from the proposal, is:

“To contribute to a measurable reduction in neonatal mortality among the served population in Sheopur, Shivpuri and Guna districts by the end of the project period.”

The programme's theory, as described in the narrative, is that newborn deaths fall when (a) more births happen in health facilities with skilled attendance, (b) families recognise and act on newborn danger signs early, and (c) ASHAs are equipped to counsel and refer. The proposal does not, however, lay out this causal chain explicitly, and the indicators in the logframe were drafted separately, under deadline pressure.

4. Implementation structure

  • A district coordinator in each of the three districts, supported by 4–5 block-level field officers.
  • Frontline delivery through the government ASHA/ANM cadre, with HealthBridge providing training, materials and supportive supervision.
  • Routine data drawn from camp registers, training records, counselling logs and the government HMIS (Health Management Information System).
  • A small M&E unit (one manager, two officers) responsible for reporting against the logframe.

5. Known constraints

  • HMIS data on neonatal deaths is incomplete in remote blocks; community-based death reporting is not yet systematic.
  • The logframe was finalised in the last week before submission and has not been revised since.
  • No baseline survey was commissioned; baseline figures (see Document 3) were assembled from HMIS, NFHS-5 and a rapid first-year review.
Your task. Using this description and the baseline data, diagnose what is wrong with the programme's current logframe and rebuild it: with level-appropriate SMART indicators, an explicit results chain to the neonatal-mortality goal, articulated assumptions, embedded learning questions, and a data-collection method for every indicator.
