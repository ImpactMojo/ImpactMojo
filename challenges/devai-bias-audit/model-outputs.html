About this extract

The Jharkhand Department of Social Welfare deployed a machine-learning model to score households for eligibility in a new direct-benefit social-protection scheme. The model ingests Socio-Economic Caste Census records, ration-card data, and satellite imagery to predict a vulnerability score on a 0–100 scale; households scoring 55 or above are flagged eligible for the priority list.

The table below is a de-identified sample of 47 households drawn from the full scoring run. Direct identifiers have been removed; household_id values are synthetic. Each row carries the model's predicted score, the binary eligibility flag, and the contextual attributes available to the audit team. The data_completeness_pct column reports the share of model input fields that were populated (versus imputed) for that household at scoring time.

Column definitions

  • household_id: synthetic anonymised identifier.
  • district: district of residence (10 districts in this extract).
  • social_group: SC (Scheduled Caste), ST (Scheduled Tribe), OBC (Other Backward Class), or General.
  • female_headed: Y if the household is headed by a woman, else N.
  • area_type: urban, rural, or forest (forest-fringe / Fifth Schedule habitation).
  • data_completeness_pct: percentage of input fields populated from source records (the remainder imputed).
  • predicted_vulnerability_score: model output, 0 (least vulnerable) to 100 (most vulnerable).
  • eligibility_flag: Y if score ≥ 55 (priority list), else N.

Field-team annotations

  • Field officers in the southern forest belt (Khunti, Gumla, Simdega, West Singhbhum) flagged that several households they considered among the poorest were returned with sub-threshold scores.
  • Data-collection centres are concentrated in district headquarters; remote habitations are surveyed less frequently, which the welfare department notes affects record freshness and completeness.
  • The model was last retrained on inputs assembled in 2018; no retraining has occurred since the pandemic.
