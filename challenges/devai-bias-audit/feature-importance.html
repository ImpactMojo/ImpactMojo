What Drives the Vulnerability Score

Vulnerability Targeting Model v2.1 · gradient-boosted tree ensemble · trained 2018

Model card (summary)

  • Architecture: gradient-boosted decision-tree ensemble (regression head, 0–100 output).
  • Training data: assembled in 2018 from SECC 2018, PDS / ration-card records, and 2017–18 satellite composites. Not retrained since.
  • Label: a 2018 expert-assigned vulnerability rating used as the supervised target.
  • Inputs: 11 features (below). Missing fields are imputed with district-level medians before scoring.
  • Importance metric: normalised split-gain, averaged across the ensemble; weights sum to 1.00.

Proxy risk is the model team’s own flag for features that may stand in for protected or location attributes rather than for need itself. Note that neither social_group nor female_headed is a model input: the model is “blind” to them by design.

Notes from the model documentation

  • Night-lights & distance-to-centre. The two GIS/satellite features were added in v2.0 to improve coverage where survey data was thin. They are the single largest and third-largest drivers of the score.
  • Imputation. When a field is missing it is filled with the district median; the model also receives data_completeness_score directly as a feature.
  • Target vintage. The 2018 expert labels predate the pandemic; informal-sector and migrant-return distress that emerged from 2020 onward is not represented in the training signal.
  • Geographic correlates. Forest-fringe Fifth Schedule habitations are, on average, farther from data centres and paved roads and register lower night-light radiance than district headquarters.
