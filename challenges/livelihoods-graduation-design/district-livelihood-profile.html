District Livelihood Profile: Katoria & Chandan Blocks

Unnati Graduation Pilot · Banka district, Bihar · 400 ultra-poor households

Prepared by: Field team, Udyam Sakhi Sansthan · Basis: hamlet visits (38 of 52 target hamlets), block-level records, JEEViKA cluster data · Status: shared with design consultant (you)
Read this alongside the budget envelope. This profile describes the economy the pathway must work within. Your asset menu, stipend timing, coaching cadence and graduation criteria should all trace back to numbers on this page or be explicitly flagged as assumptions. The funder's constraints are in Document 2: Budget Envelope.

1. The target population

The 400 households were shortlisted from 52 hamlets (tolas) across Katoria and Chandan blocks in the southern, hilly part of Banka district. Nearly all are landless or near-landless (under 0.2 acre homestead land); roughly 70% are Mahadalit (mostly Musahar and Bhuiyan communities), the rest Santhal Adivasi and extremely poor EBC households. The shortlist came from JEEViKA's participatory identification lists plus our own hamlet visits: it has not yet been verified, and both blocks have a history of contested BPL lists. How you confirm the final 400 is part of your design.

  • Seasonal out-migration of working-age men is the norm: typically November–May to brick kilns near Bhagalpur and construction work in Punjab and Surat. For 6–7 months a year, most target households are effectively women-headed.
  • Adult literacy among target-group women is low (field team estimate: under 25%); thumb-impression banking is common.
  • Most households eat two meals a day in the lean months (September–October, and again March–April before kiln wages arrive).

3. Markets and value chains

  • Haats: Katoria haat runs Tuesday and Saturday; Chandan haat runs Wednesday and Sunday. Median target hamlet is 4–7 km from the nearest haat; the farthest cluster (8 hamlets in the hills south of Katoria) is 11–14 km with no all-weather road.
  • Goats: sold at the haat to itinerant traders (paikars) who quote by eye, not weight. Field team's spot checks suggest sellers lose 15–20% versus weight-based pricing at Banka town, 25–35 km away.
  • Poultry: exactly one aggregator (a feed-and-chick dealer at Katoria block HQ) buys backyard birds and supplies chicks; he currently handles roughly 400 birds a week and says he could double volume "if supply were reliable". No cold chain anywhere in the two blocks.
  • Petty trade: the bazaars at both block HQs support small vending (vegetables, dry fish, bangles, snacks); daily net margins reported by existing vendors are ₹150–250. Rented pushcarts cost ₹40/day.
  • Mushroom: JEEViKA has trained ~30 women in oyster-mushroom cultivation in neighbouring Belhar block; spawn must currently be brought from Bhagalpur (60+ km). Local demand exists at haats but is thin; a Bounsi-road trader takes bulk lots at ₹110–130/kg.

4. Infrastructure and services

Electricity Grid reaches 47 of 52 hamlets; supply averages 9–12 hours/day, worse in the monsoon Anything needing refrigeration or reliable power is high-risk
Veterinary services One government para-vet ("pashu sakhi" coverage nil) per ~30 villages; block veterinary hospital at each HQ, chronically short of vaccines Livestock assets without an embedded vaccination/first-aid plan will fail
Banking Bank branches at both block HQs; 6 active CSP/banking correspondents; ~80% of target women have a (mostly dormant) PMJDY account Stipend and asset transfers can be digital, but expect hand-holding for first withdrawals
Roads 34 of 52 hamlets connected by all-weather road; the rest cut off for 2–6 weeks in the monsoon Coaching cadence and input delivery must survive the monsoon
SHG coverage (JEEViKA / NRLM) NRLM SHGs active in ~60% of target hamlets; in those hamlets, roughly half the target women are already members. The remaining 40% of hamlets (mostly the remote Musahar tolas) have no functioning SHG SHG linkage is a realistic graduation criterion, but only if you plan for the uncovered hamlets, in coordination with the JEEViKA block team

5. Hazard notes

Hazard What the data says Field team comment
Goat mortality (PPR, pneumonia, diarrhoea) 25–40% annual kid mortality without vaccination and deworming; block vet hospital records show PPR outbreaks in 3 of the last 5 years With a trained pashu sakhi, PPR vaccination (₹5–10/dose) and deworming, comparable JEEViKA clusters report mortality under 10%
Poultry disease (Ranikhet/Newcastle) Backyard flocks periodically wiped out; last major die-off two years ago took an estimated 60–70% of village birds Aggregator sells vaccinated chicks at ₹8 premium per bird; uptake near zero so far
Flooding (Chandan river and its nallas) 9 of the 52 target hamlets sit in flood-prone panchayats; significant inundation in 2 of the last 4 monsoons Asset choices for these 9 hamlets need a flood plan (raised night shelters for goats, timing of asset transfer outside Jun–Sep)
Distress sale of assets In a prior (non-graduation) asset scheme in the district, an estimated 20–25% of transferred goats were sold within 6 months to meet health or debt shocks This is exactly what the consumption stipend and savings components are meant to prevent: sequencing matters
