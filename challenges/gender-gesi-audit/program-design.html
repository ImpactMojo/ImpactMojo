1. Background & rationale

Grameen Shakti is a livelihoods-focused NGO working across central and eastern India. In 2023 it secured a three-year grant to raise household incomes among rural and tribal-majority communities in five blocks of the Bastar region of Chhattisgarh. These blocks have high rates of seasonal out-migration, low non-farm employment, and limited access to formal credit. Women, in particular, are heavily engaged in unpaid agricultural and care work but have few independent earning opportunities.

The programme was designed around a simple premise: if poor households are given marketable skills, access to working capital, and a route to market, they can build small enterprises that raise and diversify income. Because women dominate the available labour pool in these blocks during the lean season, the design assumes that most participants will be women, and a headline target of 70% women participants was written into the results framework.

2. Goal and objectives

The programme's stated goal, taken verbatim from the proposal, is:

“To improve and diversify the incomes of 8,000 poor rural households across five blocks of Chhattisgarh through skills, finance and market access, with women as the primary participants.”

Three programme objectives sit under this goal:

  1. Skills. Equip at least 8,000 participants with a market-relevant vocational skill.
  2. Finance. Channel micro-enterprise loans of ₹25,000–₹50,000 to at least 5,000 trained participants.
  3. Market. Link at least 3,000 enterprises to aggregators, fairs or institutional buyers.

3.1 Skills training

Three trades are offered, selected on the basis of a 2022 rapid market scan and the trades the programme could source qualified trainers for: tailoring and garment-making, backyard poultry, and mushroom cultivation. Each batch runs for 30 working days at the block-level training centre. Sessions run from 10:00 a.m. to 3:30 p.m., six days a week, to fit a full curriculum into the month. Trainees who complete 80% attendance receive a completion certificate, which is a precondition for the loan.

3.2 Micro-enterprise loans

Loans are disbursed by a partner cooperative bank. To qualify, an applicant must:

  • Hold a training-completion certificate from the programme;
  • Be aged 21–55 and a resident of the target block;
  • Produce a one-page enterprise plan vetted by the field mobiliser;
  • Provide a male guarantor (a husband, father, brother or adult son) who co-signs the loan, in line with the cooperative bank's standard lending policy for unsecured micro-enterprise credit;
  • Open or hold a savings account at the partner bank branch.

Loan sizes are tiered: ₹25,000 for a first enterprise loan, rising to ₹50,000 on successful repayment of a first cycle. Repayment is monthly over 18–24 months.

3.3 Market linkage

Trained producers are connected to buyers appropriate to their trade: garment aggregators in Jagdalpur for tailoring units, district poultry traders for egg and broiler producers, and the state mushroom mission for spawn supply and offtake. Quarterly haat fairs give producer groups a direct retail outlet.

4. Eligibility & targeting

  • Households below the state poverty line or holding a priority-household ration card are eligible.
  • One participant is enrolled per household to spread reach across the 8,000-household target.
  • The design sets a 70% women participation target and instructs mobilisers to prioritise women's enrolment.
  • Enrolment is voluntary and open; participants self-select into one of the three trades at the start of each batch.

5. Implementation structure

  • A block coordinator in each of the five blocks, each supported by 6–8 community mobilisers who handle outreach, enrolment and group formation.
  • Three contracted master trainers rotate across the block training centres by trade.
  • The partner cooperative bank handles loan appraisal, disbursement and recovery.
  • A two-person M&E cell at the head office maintains the participant database and reports against the results framework.
