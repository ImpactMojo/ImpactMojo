1. State Nutrition Survey 2025: anaemia headline numbers

The Dakshin Pradesh State Nutrition Survey (SNS 2025), run by the Directorate of Health Services with an independent academic partner, sampled 14,820 children aged 6–59 months and 11,240 adolescent girls aged 10–19 across all seven districts between January and March 2025. Anaemia is defined per WHO haemoglobin cut-offs (capillary sample, HemoCue). District-level estimates carry margins of error of roughly ±3 percentage points.

2. Millet menu pilot: Chandanagiri and Marutham, 2024–25

Under the previous government, the School Education Department piloted a revised midday meal menu in two districts: three millet-based meals per week (ragi mudde/porridge, jowar roti, foxtail millet khichdi) replacing rice-based preparations, with local SHG-run kitchens sourcing millets through the district supply co-operative. The pilot covered 1,412 schools and roughly 2.1 lakh children over two academic terms. Monitoring was done by the state SCERT with an NGO partner; there was no randomised comparison, so figures below compare pilot schools with the rest of the state over the same period.

3. Iron fortification and supplementation: precedents

  • Fortified rice in PM POSHAN. The Government of India completed scale-up of iron-folic-acid-B12 fortified rice (FRK blended at 1:100) across midday meals nationally by 2024. Dakshin Pradesh already receives fortified rice through FCI allocations; a millet-heavy menu would partially displace this fortification channel on millet days.
  • Weekly Iron and Folic Acid Supplementation (WIFS). The national programme entitles school-age children to weekly IFA tablets (blue: 10–19 years; pink: 5–9 years). Dakshin Pradesh's reported WIFS coverage was 34% of enrolled children in 2024–25, against a national median around 55%: largely a distribution and supervision problem, not a supply problem.
  • Double-fortified salt (DFS). Two neighbouring states have used iron-iodine DFS in school meals; evaluations (including a large RCT in Bihar, 2015) found modest haemoglobin effects when consumption and cooking practices were monitored, and near-zero effects when they were not.
  • State millet missions. Odisha's Shree Anna/millet mission and Karnataka's ragi-based menus are the commonly cited models for procurement architecture (decentralised procurement from farmer producer organisations at a state-declared incentive price), not for measured anaemia impact.

The state share of the existing material cost norm is 40%; any menu enhancement beyond the norm is 100% state-funded unless the centre concurs.
