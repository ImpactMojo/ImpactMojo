Free Textbook Scheme: Actor Map

Who touches the textbook file, what they gain or lose, what they know, and where they can stop the process

Source: Directorate establishment records, association filings, AG audit report 2023, interview notes of the transition team · Compiled: June 2026
These are descriptions, not conclusions. Nothing here has been labelled a problem. Read this alongside the six-year timeline (Document 1) and decide for yourself whose incentives explain what the record shows.

A. The actors in brief

Madhya Kshetra Mudran Evam Prakashan Sangh (printer association). Twelve member firms are registered on the departmental panel; membership has not changed since 2017. Three members: Saraswati Offset Pvt. Ltd., Vindhya Print & Pack, and Kamdhenu Printers: between them win around 80% of contract value each year. The Sangh's secretary attends every pre-bid meeting, files representations on specifications and lot structure, and has twice obtained High Court stays on re-tenders (2021, 2023), each stay adding 5–7 weeks to the cycle. Two of the three large firms have directors related by marriage; all three source paper through the same Indore-based supplier.

Procurement Cell, Directorate of Public Instruction. Two sanctioned posts: an Assistant Director (Procurement) and a Section Officer. Average tenure in the Cell over the last decade is 18 months; six Assistant Directors have held the post in nine years. The Cell drafts specifications, runs the tender, and answers every audit query: including queries about decisions taken by predecessors. Officials describe the posting privately as a “punishment seat”: high file volume, guaranteed audit attention, no field allowance. The one departmental honour available (a Republic Day commendation) has gone to officials who closed audit paras, not to any official for on-time delivery, which no register measures.

District Education Officers (45). Receive stock at district godowns, sign the quality certificate that releases the printer's final 20% payment, and supervise block-level distribution. No district has paper-testing equipment; the certificate format asks the DEO to certify GSM, brightness, and binding “as per specification”. A DEO who declines to sign holds up payment but must then store the disputed stock, answer the Directorate's queries about the delay, and face the printer's representation. Signing has no recorded consequence in any of the six years on file.

Finance Department. Releases the scheme budget in tranches. The first-quarter release has arrived after 15 May in five of the last six years, though the departmental budget is voted by 31 March. Finance's stated concern, recorded on file each year, is “parking of funds”, money released early that sits unspent invites its own audit observation. No Finance official has ever been asked about the downstream cost of a late release.

Teachers, parents, and students. Around 68 lakh enrolled children and their households bear the cost: teachers photocopy chapters or teach from a single copy through the first term; poorer parents buy private editions at ₹40–120 per title where available. The loss per household is real but small and diffuse; there is no parents' organisation active on textbooks, and School Management Committees have raised the issue in fewer than 2% of recorded meeting minutes. The press covers the story every July as a one-day item.

C. Note on the 2023 episode

In July 2023, the then Assistant Director (Procurement), Shri R. K. Sonwane, recorded a detailed note on file documenting binding failures in the Class VI–VIII science titles, cross-referencing the inspection agency's own sample data, and recommending (i) invocation of the penalty clause against the supplying firm and (ii) initiation of blacklisting proceedings. He also wrote to the Director suggesting the inspection agency's empanelment be re-tendered. Within eleven weeks, Shri Sonwane was transferred to the post of District Adult Education Officer, Baigarh (a non-cadre post four levels below his grade in practical significance), on “administrative grounds”. The blacklisting proposal was returned by the Law Department for “re-examination” and lapsed when his successor did not pursue it. The penalty notice was among the liquidated-damages items later waived. The Sangh's representation against Shri Sonwane, filed three weeks before his transfer, alleged “procedural harassment of bidders”; no inquiry into the representation's claims was ever completed. Officials interviewed by the transition team in 2026 referred to the episode unprompted.
