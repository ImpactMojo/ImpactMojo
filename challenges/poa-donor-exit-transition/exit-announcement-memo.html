Exit Announcement: Sajha Swasthya Partnership

Notification of portfolio consolidation and phased closure of grant VDCA-NPL-2020-014

Classification: Partner-confidential until joint communication · Received by consortium: Year 6, Q2
Read the language, not just the content. This memo is written in donor HQ register. Part of your task is to translate what is actually being offered, what is being asked, and where the negotiable space is. The programme factsheet (Document 1 of 2) gives you the numbers.
FROM: Department for Asia and Global Health, Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the Kingdom of Veldenmark
THROUGH: Embassy of the Kingdom of Veldenmark, Kathmandu
TO: Country Director, HealthWorks International (fiscal agent, Sajha Swasthya Consortium)
CC: Secretary, Ministry of Health, Madhesh Province; Director, Provincial Health Directorate, Janakpurdham
REF: VDCA/AGH/2026/0417 · SUBJECT: Strategic reprioritisation of the Nepal portfolio, consolidation and responsible completion of the Sajha Swasthya partnership

1. Following the conclusion of the Ministry's global strategy review, Partnership for Impact 2030, the Government of Veldenmark has decided to consolidate its development portfolio around fewer, larger engagements in climate adaptation and green energy transition. As part of this strategic reprioritisation, bilateral health programming in Nepal will be brought to a responsible completion.

2. Accordingly, grant VDCA-NPL-2020-014 (Sajha Swasthya Community Health Partnership) will close 18 months from the date of this letter. The Ministry recognises the partnership's considerable achievements and is committed to an orderly transition that consolidates gains, honours our mutual commitments, and secures the legacy and lesson-learning value of six years of collaboration.

What the Ministry is prepared to offer

  1. One no-cost extension of up to six months may be considered for completion activities only, subject to a satisfactory transition plan; it does not alter the disbursement ceiling above.
  2. Transfer of programme assets to government or consortium entities per standard procedures (Annex K of the grant agreement), on receipt of a countersigned asset register and disposal plan.
  3. A final independent evaluation, commissioned and managed by the Ministry, to document results and distil lessons for the global health portfolio.

What the Ministry requests of the consortium

  1. A costed transition and completion plan within 90 days, prepared jointly with the Provincial Health Directorate, identifying which functions transfer to government, which are localised, and which are concluded.
  2. A joint communication plan, agreed before any public announcement, presenting the partnership's closure as the planned maturation of a successful engagement. Messaging should emphasise achievements and national ownership.
  3. All programme datasets anonymised and archived with the Ministry's evidence unit in Veldenmark by month 15, in accordance with the grant agreement's data provisions.
  4. No new activities, no new hires, and no contract extensions beyond the closure date without prior written approval.

3. The Embassy stands ready to facilitate a first transition dialogue at the earliest convenience of the consortium and the Provincial Government. We are confident that, together, we will bring this valued partnership to a dignified and well-documented conclusion.

(signed)
Deputy Director-General, Department for Asia and Global Health

Attached: internal note circulated with this memo

INTERNAL: CONSORTIUM SENIOR MANAGEMENT ONLY
FROM: Dr. Sarita Jha, Consortium Director, Janakpurdham · TO: SMT + board chairs, three partner NGOs

Colleagues: read the memo twice before you react; the language is warmer than the arithmetic. Four things keep me up at night. First, the FCHV network committee in Dhanusha has already told us they will suspend monthly reporting if top-ups stop, and a reporting blackout in months 7–12 will be read in Veldenmark not as protest but as proof the programme was donor-scaffolding all along, which becomes the story of the final evaluation. Second, the Directorate will take the vehicles and the three HMIS staff and decline the top-ups, and it will be me, not the donor and not the province, standing in front of 340 women in a hall in Jaleshwar explaining why the part that touched them is the part nobody would pay for. Third, nobody has costed severance: 23 contracts written against a Year 10 horizon, and the 60% and 30% tranches must fund both wind-down and handover, if we sequence this badly we will be choosing between paying our staff their dues and financing the transition itself. Fourth, the “joint success-story communication plan” asks me to sign the donor's narrative before we have negotiated the exit terms: once we have publicly called this a success and a planned maturation, we have surrendered every ounce of leverage the truth still gives us, and the communities who hear us say it will remember who said it. I want options, not eulogies, by Friday.: S.J.
