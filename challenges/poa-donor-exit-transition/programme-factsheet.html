Sajha Swasthya Consortium · Programme Factsheet

Sajha Swasthya Community Health Partnership

Community health systems · 14 partner palikas across Dhanusha & Mahottari districts, Madhesh Province, Nepal

Prepared: Year 6, Q2 · For: consortium board & provincial coordination committee · Status: current as of the donor's exit announcement
This factsheet describes the programme as it stands today. Read it alongside the donor's exit announcement memo (Document 2 of 2). Your task in the challenge is to design the 18-month transition, not to defend the programme's past.

1. The programme in one paragraph

Sajha Swasthya (“shared health”) is a ten-year community health partnership launched in 2020 between the Veldenmark Development Cooperation Agency (VDCA, the bilateral agency of the Kingdom of Veldenmark), the Madhesh Provincial Health Directorate in Janakpurdham, and a three-member NGO consortium: Janaki Health Foundation (Nepali NGO, consortium lead for field delivery), Mithila Samudayik Bikas Samaj (Nepali NGO), and HealthWorks International (INGO, holder of the grant agreement and fiscal agent). It works in 8 gaunpalikas and 6 nagarpalikas, supporting the federal Female Community Health Volunteer (FCHV) programme with monthly incentive top-ups, running a pooled referral transport fund for obstetric and under-five emergencies, and staffing a three-person HMIS strengthening cell embedded in the Provincial Health Directorate.

2. Budget history

5. The sustainability clauses everyone ignored

The 2020 Programme Design Document, Section 7 (“Sustainability and Transition”), was signed by all parties, including the Provincial Health Directorate. Its clauses have never been actioned:

§7.2: From Year 4, partner palikas shall co-finance FCHV incentive top-ups at 25%, rising to 50% by Year 6 and 100% by Year 8, budgeted through the palika red book (annual budget). Status: zero palikas have budgeted a co-financing line. Three palika chairs say they were never briefed.
§7.3: The three HMIS cell positions shall be converted to sanctioned provincial posts (darbandi) by the end of Year 5, subject to Provincial Public Service Commission processes. Status: the conversion file has been in the provincial Ministry of Health since 2023; no darbandi created.
§7.4: A joint transition review shall be convened at midterm to agree a phased handover calendar. Status: the midterm review (Year 5) flagged this clause as off-track; no transition review was ever convened.
§7.5: An asset register with agreed end-of-programme disposition (transfer, sale, or return) shall be updated annually and countersigned by the Directorate. Status: register exists but was last countersigned in 2021; the six vehicles are its most contested entries.

6. Where things stand now

  • The 340 FCHVs, coordinated informally through an FCHV network committee in Dhanusha, have written to the consortium: if top-ups stop, they will suspend monthly reporting (their statutory role is voluntary; the top-up is the only cash they receive besides federal dress allowance and travel reimbursements).
  • The Provincial Health Directorate has verbally requested the six vehicles and the absorption of the three HMIS staff, and has stated it “cannot and should not” take on recurring top-up payments it considers a palika or federal responsibility.
  • Palika chairs are split: four are open to co-financing top-ups if phased; the rest cite unfunded federal mandates and the upcoming local elections.
  • The consortium employs 23 staff whose contracts run to the original Year 10; severance exposure has not been calculated.
