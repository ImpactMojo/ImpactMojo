1. District context

Muzaffarpur is a large, predominantly rural district in north Bihar within the Tirhut division, with a population of roughly 4.8 million across 16 community-development blocks. Agriculture and seasonal labour migration dominate livelihoods; the district is flood-prone along the Burhi Gandak and Bagmati rivers. Female literacy (around 60%) trails male literacy by a wide margin, and adolescent girls are a large but under-served group: an estimated 245,000 girls aged 15–19, of whom a substantial share are out of school by age 16.

3. Anemia & nutrition burden

Adolescent-girl anemia in Muzaffarpur (68%) sits above the all-India average and reflects a long pipeline of under-nutrition: low dietary diversity, gendered intra-household food allocation, menstrual blood loss without iron replacement, and a high worm/infection load. The burden compounds into pregnancy: many girls enter early marriage and childbearing already anemic.

5. Relevant programmes & entitlements

A campaign does not start from zero: several national programmes already mandate adolescent anemia services. The gap is uptake, reach and adolescent-friendliness, not policy.

Anemia Mukt Bharat (AMB)

National anemia strategy using the “6x6x6” framework (six interventions, six beneficiary groups, six institutional mechanisms). For adolescents it mandates prophylactic iron-folic acid, deworming, and intensified IEC. District AMB dashboards exist but supply and counselling are weak.

Weekly Iron and Folic Acid Supplementation (WIFS)

Provides one supervised blue iron-folic-acid tablet per week to adolescents 10–19, plus biannual deworming. Delivered through schools (for enrolled adolescents) and Anganwadi Centres (for out-of-school girls). In Muzaffarpur, school delivery is partial and the AWC channel for out-of-school girls is largely dormant: hence weekly coverage of only ~19%.

Rashtriya Kishor Swasthya Karyakram (RKSK)

The national adolescent health programme. Its instruments include Adolescent-Friendly Health Clinics, trained Peer Educators (Saathiya), and Adolescent Health Days at AWCs/sub-centres. In the district these instruments are under-deployed: only two AFHCs function, peer educators are sparse, and Adolescent Health Days are irregular.

Other touchpoints

  • ICDS / Anganwadi & POSHAN Abhiyaan: nutrition platform reaching mothers and young children, potential, under-used channel to out-of-school adolescent girls.
  • Mid-Day Meal (PM POSHAN): school meals currently lack deliberately iron-rich/fortified options.
  • ASHA cadre: ~3,600 workers, but incentives and routines are built around maternal and child health, not adolescents.

6. Key gaps a campaign must navigate

  • No adolescent-specific service point for most girls: only 2 functional AFHCs district-wide.
  • ASHA bandwidth and mandate are oriented to maternal health; reaching adolescents needs new routines or incentives.
  • The out-of-school channel (AWC/WIFS) is dormant, leaving the worst-off girls unreached.
  • Schools lack structured health education, so even enrolled girls get little behaviour-change support.
  • Supply reliability of WIFS tablets is uneven: demand-generation messaging must not outrun stock.
Your task. Treat these programmes and assets as the system layer of your campaign. A strong design will not just “raise awareness”, it will route messages and behaviours through AMB, WIFS, RKSK and the AWC/school/ASHA platforms, and surface the systems-level changes (e.g. activating the AWC channel for out-of-school girls) the District Health Society would need to make for the campaign to work.
