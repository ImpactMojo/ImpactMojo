Behaviour-Change Communication Strategy Template

A reusable, fill-in scaffold structured on the socio-ecological model

How to use this document. This is one of three case files for the challenge “Design a BCC Campaign for Adolescent Nutrition”. It is a blank planning scaffold: copy each section into your submission and fill it in using the KAP Survey Summary and District Health Profile. Italic grey text is guidance to replace, not content to keep.
Core principle. Behaviour change rarely fails for lack of information. It fails because the desired behaviour is hard, socially costly, or unsupported by the system. A strong BCC strategy works at every level of the socio-ecological model (individual, household, community, and systems) not just by “raising awareness.”

Framing tip. Reframe iron supplementation away from “sickness” toward something aspirational and positive: energy, concentration, strength, confidence. Use trusted, relatable messengers (peer educators, a respected older girl, a sympathetic AWW) rather than clinical authority alone.

5. Channel mix by socio-ecological level

Map channels to levels and audiences. A channel is only as good as the audience it actually reaches: remember that schools cannot reach out-of-school girls.

Individual level

Knowledge, attitudes and self-efficacy of the girl herself. Channels: peer educators (Saathiya), one-to-one counselling, school sessions, interactive content, IPC tools. Fill in which channels, for which segment, and the key message each carries.

Household level

The people who decide what she eats and whether she takes a tablet: mothers, mothers-in-law, fathers. Channels: home visits, mothers’ groups, AWC contacts, household-targeted narratives. Fill in.

Community level

Norms, taboos and collective permission. Channels: community radio, folk media / street theatre (nukkad natak), wall paintings, Adolescent Health Days, SHG/VHSND platforms, religious or panchayat endorsement. Fill in.

Systems / structural level

Supply, service and policy that must be in place for the behaviour to be possible. Levers: activating the AWC/WIFS channel for out-of-school girls, ASHA orientation & incentives for adolescents, reliable tablet supply, functioning AFHCs, iron-rich/fortified school meals. Indicator tip. Pair at least one demand indicator (uptake) with one supply indicator (availability). If demand rises but tablets are out of stock, the campaign backfires: your monitoring should catch that early.
