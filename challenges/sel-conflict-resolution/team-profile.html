1. About the team

Samarth Foundation runs rural livelihoods and water-sanitation-hygiene (WASH) programmes across two Garhwal-region districts of Uttarakhand. The field team of 15 is the front line of delivery: community mobilisers, field officers, two senior field coordinators, a small monitoring-and-data pair, an accounts/admin person, and the team lead who reports to the head office in Dehradun.

Until eight months ago the work was run as two separate district teams: one anchored in Pauri Garhwal (largely the livelihoods programme) and one in Tehri Garhwal (largely WASH). In a recent reorganisation the two teams were merged into a single field unit reporting to one team lead, with the intention of sharing staff across both programmes. The two groups had developed quite different working cultures: the Pauri team was used to informal, verbal coordination, while the Tehri team ran on written plans and fixed weekly review meetings. They are now expected to operate as one team.

4. A neutral summary of current tensions

The following observations were gathered by the team lead and head office during routine check-ins and a recent staff pulse-check. They are presented as what people reported feeling or observing, without attributing fault or proposing a fix.

Between the two senior coordinators

  • The two senior field coordinators (#2 and #3) have not spoken to each other directly for about three months. When something needs coordinating across the two programmes, they pass messages through junior staff or the M&E officer.
  • Each has described the other, in private, as “difficult to work with.” The specific trigger is unclear; staff trace it to a disagreement over shared vehicle and field-day scheduling after the merger.

Between senior and junior staff

  • Several junior staff (field officers and mobilisers) say their ideas are dismissed or talked over in team meetings, and that decisions feel pre-made by the senior coordinators.
  • Some junior staff have stopped raising suggestions; one mobiliser noted she now “just waits to be told what to do.”

Between the two origin teams

  • The merger brought together two groups with different working styles: one more informal and verbal, one more structured and written. Each finds the other's style frustrating.
  • Staff from the two districts hold stereotypes about each other's work ethic: e.g. one group is described by the other as “slow and over-cautious,” and in return as “disorganised and always improvising.” These labels are repeated half-jokingly but have started to stick.

The team lead's stance

  • The team lead (#1) is relatively new (10 months) and tends to avoid raising the conflicts directly, hoping they will settle on their own as people get used to the merger.
  • Meetings stay focused on tasks and deadlines; the underlying relationships are not discussed.

5. Constraints the workshop must respect

ConstraintDetail
TimeHalf a day only: about 3 hours, including any breaks.
ResourcesNo projector or screen. Flipcharts, paper and markers are the only reliable materials. The venue is a community hall with movable chairs.
LanguageMixed Hindi/Garhwali team. Some juniors are far more comfortable in Garhwali than in formal Hindi. Activities should not disadvantage anyone by language.
CultureHierarchy and respect for seniority are strong; people may be reluctant to speak openly in front of senior colleagues. Direct public criticism is likely to feel unsafe.
TrustThis is a one-off half-day; trust is currently low, especially between the two coordinators. Anything that feels like “being put on the spot” may backfire.
