Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana (PMGSY): How It Works

What the scheme does, who runs it, and which records a citizen can ask for

Study summary. This brief describes the general design of PMGSY, India's rural roads programme launched in 2000, so you know what documents exist and where to look. Detailed cost norms, thresholds and procedures are set out in the official PMGSY Operations Manual and successive programme guidelines and may have been updated; verify current figures against the official guidelines before relying on them.
How to use this document. This is one of three case files for the challenge “Draft an RTI Strategy for an Accountability Campaign”. Read it with the RTI Act key sections and the sample RTI application format. Use Section 7 (“Records a citizen can request”) to build Jan Sarokar's itemised, phased information requests.

1. What PMGSY is

The Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana is a Centrally Sponsored Scheme of the Ministry of Rural Development whose core objective is to provide a single all-weather road connection to eligible unconnected rural habitations above a population threshold. Roads are built to prescribed engineering specifications, with later phases adding upgradation of existing routes (PMGSY-II) and roads in Left-Wing-Extremism-affected and border areas. Funding is shared between the Centre and the States in a defined ratio, and the programme is implemented by State agencies but monitored nationally.

2. Who runs it (the institutions)

LevelBodyRole
NationalNational Rural Infrastructure Development Agency (NRIDA), Ministry of Rural DevelopmentSets guidelines, releases funds, runs the online monitoring system, commissions independent quality monitoring
StateState Rural Roads Development Agency (SRRDA)Receives funds, maintains accounts, awards and administers contracts in the State
District / BlockProgramme Implementation Unit (PIU): usually under the State Rural Works / Rural Engineering DepartmentPrepares estimates, supervises construction, certifies measurements and payments on the ground
VillageGram Panchayat / habitationBeneficiary; a citizen group like Jan Sarokar engages at this level
Why this matters for RTI. Different records sit with different bodies. Contract and payment records and quality reports are typically with the PIU / SRRDA; muster rolls and measurement books are at the PIU / executing division; sanction and fund-release records may be at State or NRIDA level. File with the PIU first; if a PIO does not hold the record, s.6(3) of the RTI Act obliges them to transfer the request to the authority that does.

3. Sanctioning a road (the paper trail begins)

The route from “identified habitation” to “sanctioned work” generates records at each step:

  1. Core Network & survey: habitations and routes are identified; a Detailed Project Report (DPR) is prepared with alignment, surveys, drawings and a detailed cost estimate.
  2. Scrutiny & technical sanction: the DPR is checked (including by a State Technical Agency) and given technical sanction.
  3. Administrative approval / clearance: the work is cleared at the State Empowered Committee / Ministry level and the road appears in the sanctioned works list with a sanctioned cost.
  4. Online entry: the sanctioned work, its cost and progress are entered on the PMGSY online monitoring portal, much of which is already public.

4. Contracts & tendering

PMGSY works are executed by contractors selected through competitive (usually e-) tendering. The process produces a Notice Inviting Tender (NIT), bid documents, a record of bids received, a comparative statement, the evaluation / award decision, and a signed agreement with a Bill of Quantities (BoQ), rates, time-frame and defect-liability conditions. Each contract carries a stipulated completion period and a defect-liability / maintenance period (commonly five years) during which the same contractor is responsible for routine maintenance, which is directly relevant to a road that develops potholes within six months.

Conflict of interest. Tender rules require disclosure of relationships and bar collusion and conflicts of interest. Jan Sarokar's report that the contractor is a relative of the Block Development Officer is exactly the kind of allegation the tender record (bidder list, evaluation, award note, any conflict-of-interest declarations) can help test.

5. Quality control: the three-tier monitoring system

PMGSY is known for a structured, three-tier quality assurance regime. Records exist at every tier:

TierWhoWhat they record
Tier 1PIU's own engineers / contractor's process controlDay-to-day field and laboratory quality control during construction; mandatory tests entered in the prescribed quality-control registers / format
Tier 2State Quality Monitors (SQMs) appointed by the StatePeriodic independent inspection of ongoing and completed works; graded inspection reports (e.g. Satisfactory / Unsatisfactory)
Tier 3National Quality Monitors (NQMs) deployed by NRIDAIndependent national-level inspection; reports and grades fed into the monitoring system; action sought on adverse grades

In addition, completion is certified through measurement and a completion certificate, and funds are released against measured, verified quantities. A road “complete on paper” will have a completion certificate and final payment: documents that can be set against what is physically on the ground (which a citizen may inspect under RTI s.2(j)).

6. Muster rolls, measurement & payments

Construction generates a chain of financial records, each verifiable against the others:

  • Muster roll: the daily record of labour engaged on the work, names, attendance and wages paid. “Ghost workers” (names on the muster roll who did not work, or do not exist) are detected by cross-checking muster rolls against job cards, bank/wage payment records and the actual quantum of work.
  • Measurement Book (MB): the field record of work actually measured and recorded by the engineer, the legal basis for payment. Measured quantities should reconcile with the BoQ and with what is physically built.
  • Running & final bills: contractor bills paid against measured quantities, with deductions; the final bill closes the contract.
  • Cash book / vouchers: the SRRDA/PIU accounting record of releases and payments.
Triangulation. The strongest accountability cases come from comparing records: sanctioned cost vs. final payment; BoQ quantities vs. Measurement Book vs. physical road; muster-roll labour vs. wage-payment records; completion certificate date vs. quality-inspection grades. Design the RTI requests so the answers can be cross-checked, not just collected.

8. Glossary

  • NRIDA: National Rural Infrastructure Development Agency (national programme body)
  • SRRDA: State Rural Roads Development Agency
  • PIU: Programme Implementation Unit (district/block-level executing office)
  • DPR: Detailed Project Report
  • BoQ: Bill of Quantities
  • MB: Measurement Book
  • SQM / NQM: State / National Quality Monitor
  • BDO: Block Development Officer
