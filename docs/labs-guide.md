# Studios Guide

## What Are the ImpactMojo Studios?

ImpactMojo offers **35 interactive studios**: hands-on workbenches where you build, design, and practice real development skills. Unlike courses (which teach concepts) or games (which simulate dynamics), studios produce **tangible outputs** (a Theory of Change diagram, a MEL plan, a policy brief) that you can export and use in your actual work.

All studios are **free, browser-based, and require no login**.

## How Studios Work

Each studio follows a structured, step-by-step process:

1. **Context setting**: you describe your programme, target group, and objectives
2. **Guided building**: the studio walks you through each component with prompts and examples
3. **Output generation**: your work is assembled into a formatted, exportable document
4. **Export**: download as PDF or PNG for use in proposals, reports, and presentations

Studios are designed to be completed in **30–60 minutes**, though you can save progress and return later.

## How Educators Can Use the Studios

### As Workshop Exercises

Studios are ready-made workshop activities. Participants work through a studio using their own programme as the case study, producing an output they can actually use.

**Example: Theory of Change Workshop (2 hours)**
1. Brief intro to ToC concepts (20 minutes)
2. Participants open the Theory of Change Studio (5 minutes)
3. Guided building: facilitator walks the room while participants work (60 minutes)
4. Pairs share and critique each other's ToCs (20 minutes)
5. Plenary discussion on common challenges (15 minutes)

### As Course Assignments

Assign a studio as a graded deliverable. The exported output serves as the submission: it's a real work product, not an exam answer.

**Good assignment pairings:**
- MEL course → MEL Studio
- Policy course → Policy & Advocacy Studio
- Programme design course → Theory of Change Studio + Risk and Mitigation Studio

### As Team Planning Tools

Use studios during actual programme planning meetings. The structured prompts help teams think through components they might otherwise skip.

### For Portfolio Building

Participants can collect studio outputs across multiple sessions to build a professional portfolio showing their ability to design ToCs, MEL frameworks, risk registers, and policy briefs.

## Getting Started

1. **Start with the Theory of Change Studio**: it's the most widely applicable and gives you a feel for how studios work
2. **Use your own programme** as the case study: the output will be immediately useful
3. **Export your work**: every studio produces a downloadable PDF or PNG
4. **Try 2–3 studios** to see how they build on each other (e.g., ToC → MEL Studio is a natural progression)

---

## Tips

- **Studios produce real outputs.** Unlike simulations, what you build in a studio is something you can include in a proposal or report. Treat it as real work, not practice.
- **Facilitators should complete each studio first.** Know what the prompts ask and where participants might get stuck before running a workshop.
- **Pair studios with courses.** The MEL course teaches concepts; the MEL Studio applies them. Using both together reinforces learning.
- **Group work is effective.** Have 2–3 participants collaborate on one studio output: the discussion about what to include is as valuable as the output itself.
