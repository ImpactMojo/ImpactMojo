# Peer Review Guide

## What Is Peer Review?

Peer Review is a **shared workspace where learners read each other's challenge submissions, give structured feedback, and see what peers thought of their own work**. On ImpactMojo it is tied to the weekly challenges: when you submit a case analysis or assignment, other learners can review it, and you review theirs in return.

The premise on the page is simple: **reviewing others sharpens your own analysis**. Most of the learning happens when you have to read someone else's work closely and say what worked, what could be stronger, and why.

Peer Review is **free but sign-in only**: it is a personal workspace tied to your own submissions. Logged-out visitors see a friendly explainer and a set of illustrative examples rather than an abrupt redirect.

Take part at [/peer-review.html](/peer-review.html).

---

## Who It's For

Peer Review is for **signed-in ImpactMojo learners** who are working through the weekly challenges. You need a free account, because the workspace is built around your own submissions and the reviews you give and receive. If you are not signed in, you can still read the illustrative examples, then [create a free account](/signup.html) or [browse open courses](/courses/) instead.

---

## How It Works

The workspace is organised into **three tabs**:

- **Review queue**: submissions by *other* learners that you have not yet reviewed. You cannot review your own work, and you can review each submission only once.
- **Reviews I've given**: a record of every review you have written, with your ratings and comments.
- **Feedback on my work**: reviews other learners have left on your submissions, grouped by submission, with average ratings.

To review a submission, open it from the queue, click **Write a review**, and fill in the form. Every review has **three 1–5 ratings (Clarity, Rigor, and Usefulness) plus written feedback**. All three ratings and the written comment are required before you can submit. Once submitted, the card leaves your queue.

On the **Feedback on my work** tab, each of your submissions shows its reviews along with **average Clarity, Rigor, and Usefulness scores** and a review count. Submissions with no reviews yet are clearly marked.

---

## The Ratings

Every review scores a submission on three dimensions, each from 1 to 5 stars:

- **Clarity**: is the submission unambiguous and easy to follow?
- **Rigor**: is the reasoning sound and the method defensible?
- **Usefulness**: would the feedback or the work actually help in practice?

The written feedback is prompted with *"What worked, what could be stronger, and why."*: the emphasis is on honest, specific, constructive notes rather than a grade.

## Worked Examples

The page shows **illustrative examples** of strong submissions and the feedback they draw, so you can see the standard before signing in. These are drawn from monitoring-and-evaluation practice, and include:

- **Design one outcome indicator**: a maternal-nutrition IFA programme, with a proposed anaemia-prevalence outcome indicator, its numerator and denominator, data source, and an attribution caveat. Peer reviews probe a selection effect in the denominator and push for a pre-registered Hb cutoff.
- **Test an assumption in your Theory of Change**: an evidence plan for whether community health workers conduct home visits, pairing each assumption with what would confirm *and* refute it, plus a numeric decision rule. Reviewers flag that an SMS/IVR poll under-represents no-phone households.
- **Draft a sampling approach for a baseline**: a two-stage PPS cluster sample for a livelihoods baseline, powered with a design effect for ICC. Reviewers note the self-weighting design and suggest sanity-checking the ICC assumption.

Each example carries two peer reviews with the same three star ratings and a few honest lines: exactly the format you use once signed in.

---

## Rules and Guardrails

- You **can't review your own submissions**.
- You can review each submission **once**: duplicates are rejected.
- Reviews require **all three ratings and written feedback**.
- The queue only shows other learners' work; your feedback and given reviews stay private to your account.
