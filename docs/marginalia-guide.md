# Marginalia Guide

## What Is Marginalia?

Marginalia is ImpactMojo's series of **cartoon essays on development work**: the gap between what the sector says and what the field replies. Every cartoon runs the same setup: someone official says the polished thing the development sector likes to say, and someone who does the real work (a field officer, an ASHA, a farmer, a beneficiary) says what usually goes unsaid.

The premise is simple: **the jokes are drawn, but every footnote is real.** Each drawing pairs the sector's claim with the field's reply, and then backs that reply up with cited, evidence-based paragraphs. It is a companion in spirit to the **Between the Logframes** podcast.

Marginalia is **free, browser-based, and requires no login.** Browse the series at [/specials/marginalia/](/specials/marginalia/).

---

## The Reading Experience

Each essay is a single scrolling page. A drawing sits beside a short written passage, numbered down the page like panels in a strip:

- **The exchange**: the claim on top, the field's reply underneath, styled as a two-line back-and-forth.
- **The commentary**: two or three paragraphs that explain why the reply holds up, with **superscript footnotes** linking to the evidence.
- **The references**: a numbered list at the foot of each essay, so the punchline lands and then holds up under a citation.

The drawings are blue-ink cartoons on a notebook-paper palette; the whole series reads like an annotated margin: hence the name. It works in light or dark theme and is fully keyboard-navigable.

## The Essays

The series currently collects **five essays**, fifty drawings in all, ten cartoons per essay, every one cited:

- **Capacity for Irony** *(Essay 1)*. The language of help meets the people it claims to help. Capacity-building, scale, pilots, logframes, "mainstreamed" gender, the last mile: each cliché answered by the frontline worker who already knew better.
- **The Fine Print** *(Essay 2)*, Every promise has a clause the slide leaves out: inclusion with stairs, ownership without the budget, evidence that never reaches the allocation, resilience without reducing the shock. The headline, and the small type underneath.
- **The Indicator Ate the Village** *(Essay 3)*, Monitoring & evaluation meets the people it measures: the baseline that learns nothing, the dashboard at 87% beside an empty clinic, real-time data for whom, the endline that never saw the year after exit, and accountability that only points up.
- **The Beneficiary Is Not a Job Title** *(Essay 4)*, The sector's vocabulary meets the people it files under it, sensitised, hard-to-reach, vulnerable, captured, amplified, people-centred. Each label is answered by the person wearing it, and footnoted with the evidence behind the reply.
- **The Theory of Change** *(Essay 5, newest)*, The logic-model industry meets the district it diagrams: the theory of change drawn after the plan, the logframe built for control, the arrows that don't survive the field, the assumptions in six-point font, attribution vs the monsoon, the measurable displacing the meaningful, systems change in a two-year grant, and adaptation the contract won't allow. Each planning-tool cliché answered by the field.

Each lives at its own page under `/specials/` (e.g. [/specials/capacity-for-irony/](/specials/capacity-for-irony/)), and all five are linked from the series index.

## How Marginalia Grows

Marginalia grows a few cartoons at a time. Each new essay collects **ten drawings around a single theme** and pairs every one with two or three cited paragraphs. New essays are added to the grid on the series index as they are finished: the "More on the way" note signals that the collection is open-ended, not a fixed set.
